Taking shade for a moment, then moving on…
when the shadow of this world becomes a path to the Hereafter

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"What do I have to do with this world? My relationship with the world is like that of a rider who seeks shade under a tree, then moves on and leaves it."

How eloquent is this prophetic image, and how deeply it impacts a heart that has grown so accustomed to this world that it mistakenly considered it its home, loving its shade so much that it forgot that departure awaits!

These are few words, yet they open a wide window in the heart unto the truth of man, the truth of this world, and the truth of the entire journey.

A rider passing along the path…

He did not build a home under the tree, nor did he attach his heart to it, nor did he say: This is my home, and I shall never leave it.

Rather, he was a traveler who knew he was a traveler.

Exhausted by the journey…

He took shade.

Overwhelmed by thirst…

He drank.

Then he looked at the path…

And he stood up and moved on.

He took shade for a moment… then moved on.

And herein lies the entire lesson.

Why did the Prophet ﷺ compare this world to the shade of a tree?

Because shade is not a home.

Shade is a temporary rest for the traveler, not a permanent place of residence.

It is a blessing as long as man recognizes its true value, and a trial if he imagines it to be his ultimate destination.

The sun on the path exhausts the body, and the shade relieves it.

But the tree does not travel with the traveler.

The moment does not stop for him.

And the road does not end beneath it.

It is as if this world is saying to man:

Take from me what assists you on your journey, but do not make me the end of your road.

This is the wisdom that is lost when the means turns into the end, when the shade turns into a homeland, the brief rest into a permanent residence, and a mere phase into the final destination.

How many people have built their entire lives under the tree of this world!

Amassing wealth…

Building homes…

Acquiring vehicles…

Chasing trade…

Attaching themselves to high positions…

Seeking praise and prestige…

Exhausting themselves behind the eyes and words of people…

Then forgetting the ultimate question:

Where am I going?

O son of Adam…

You are on the road, whether you realize it or not.

Your life passes just as a traveler passes along the path.

A day goes by, never to return.

An hour expires, never to be bought back.

Youth fades, never to come back.

Strength diminishes.

The face changes.

Friends drift apart.

And doors that were open yesterday become closed today.

Then comes the day when a person says, even if only in the silence of his heart:

The time for departure has arrived.

Only then does he realize that everything he thought was a permanent residence was nothing more than a temporary rest stop.

Taking shade for a moment, then moving on.

What a magnificent image!

The Hadith did not say: This world is entirely fire.

Nor did it say: There is no comfort in this world.

Rather, it placed shade within it.

And this is remarkably precise.

For in this world there is comfort, joy, family, wealth, health, security, and beautiful moments that no believer denies.

A person rejoices in his child.

Finds happiness with his family.

Finds comfort with a righteous friend.

Sees the fruits of his labor.

Earns a lawful living.

Rejoices at the rain.

And takes hope in a new morning.

Yet all of this is merely shade.

And shade, no matter how long it stretches, never becomes a home.

Therefore, the problem is not that you possess the world, but rather that the world possesses your heart.

That wealth is in your hand, not in your heart.

That you reside in the house, not that the house resides in your soul.

That you utilize position, not that position enslaves you.

That you benefit from health, without forgetting your inherent weakness.

That you rejoice in blessings, without forgetting the Bestower of blessings.

Be in this world like a rider under a tree.

If he finds shade, he gives thanks.

If he finds water, he drinks.

If he finds food, he eats.

If he finds wealth, he spends it in goodness.

If he finds prestige, he supports the oppressed.

If he finds strength, he assists the weak.

Then he does not forget that he is a traveler.

The greatest danger is for the traveler to forget the road because of the beauty of the rest stop.

He might find a pleasant gathering under the tree,

He might find companions,

He might hear laughter,

He might find food and drink,

And so he says:

I will stay a little longer…

Then a little longer…

Until he forgets that he was merely a passerby.

Such is this world.

It comes to you in the form of a house, and says: Stay.

It comes to you in the form of money, and says: Gather more.

It comes to you in the form of fame, and says: Do not let anyone forget you.

It comes to you in the form of position, and says: Hold onto it even if you oppress.

It comes to you in the form of desire, and says: This is true happiness.

Until the traveler loses sight of his destination.

"Know that the life of this world is but amusement and diversion and adornment and boasting one to another and competition in increase of wealth and children."
"And the worldly life is not but enjoyment of delusion."

This does not mean that you should flee from life, abandon work, forsake your family, or deprive yourself of good things.

No.

Rather, the meaning is to live in it with the heart of a traveler.

You work… but you do not forget Allah.

You gather… but you know that your hand will one day open and leave everything behind.

You build… but you know that someone else may inhabit it after you.

You plant… but you await the true harvest over there.

This world is a farm, not the harvest.

This world is a path, not the arrival.

This world is shade, not a homeland.

This world is a bridge, not a permanent abode.

This world is a journey, not the final end.

How many a house was thought by its owner to be eternal, yet after his death became a home for another!

How many a chair was occupied by an arrogant man, only for someone else to sit on it after him!

How many a fortune was gathered by its owner night and day, only to leave it in an instant, taking nothing with him except what he sent forth for the sake of Allah!

How many a person was yesterday among his family, laughing and talking, only to become today a story being told, a picture being looked at, and a name being mentioned!

This is not a call to sadness…

Rather, it is a call to wake up.

For the believer does not despise life; rather, he knows its true weight.

He takes from it what benefits him.

And leaves what harms him.

He loves, but for the sake of Allah.

He possesses, but is not enslaved.

He works, but does not forget the ultimate destiny.

He takes shade, but does not settle under the tree.

It is the beautiful equation:

Take your provisions from this world, but do not make it your ultimate goal.

If a blessing comes to you, do not say: This is my good fortune.

Say:

This is by the grace of my Lord.

If a hardship comes to you, do not say: Life is over.

Say:

I am a traveler, and this is merely a station on the road.

And if you lose something, remember that a traveler does not grieve long over an item dropped from his luggage if he knows that a far better home lies ahead of him.

A person may live fifty, sixty, or seventy years, yet when he looks back at his life, he finds it as if it were a mere flash.

Infancy…

Youth…

Adulthood…

Old age…

Then departure.

As if all of life is saying:

I took shade for a little while… then I moved on.

So O you whose mind is burdened by the worries of this world…

Lighten the weight on your heart.

O you who are exhausted by comparisons…

Stop looking at the shadows of others.

O you who have been corrupted by greed…

Remember that the tree you take shade under today, you will leave tomorrow.

O you who are saddened by the loss of something from this world…

Remember that a traveler does not weep long over a seat he left behind at a station.

O you who are deceived by your health…

Remember that the road does not become longer just because you are strong.

O you who are deceived by your wealth…

Remember that the shroud has no pockets.

O you who are deceived by your position…

Remember that the grave knows no minister, no rich man, and no poor man.

Over there…

Titles fall away.

Wealth falls away.

Prestige falls away.

Images fall away.

Appearances fall away.

And only one thing remains:

What have you sent forth?

Here, man understands the secret behind that grand prophetic counsel.

"What do I have to do with this world?"

It is not the statement of someone desperate with life, but rather the statement of one who recognized its true worth.

It is not the statement of someone who hates blessings, but rather the statement of one who does not worship the blessing.

It is not the statement of someone who abandons action, but rather the statement of one who makes action a means to meet Allah.

It is the statement of a person who viewed this world from above, and was thus not deceived by its embellishments.

He saw it as a tree along a long road.

So he took shade.

And he gave thanks.

Then he stood up.

So glad tidings to the one who realized he was a traveler before departure surprised him.

Glad tidings to the one who made his home in this world a place of rest, while his heart remained attached to the Hereafter.

Glad tidings to the one whom this world, when it gave to him, did not make him arrogant, and when it took from him, did not break him.

Glad tidings to the one who made his wealth a provision, his deeds a light, his obedience a companion, and the remembrance of Allah comfort for his heart.

O Lord…

Do not make this world our greatest concern.

Nor let its shadow make us forget our true home.

When we take shade under Your blessings, inspire us to thank You.

When You honor us, do not let Your generosity be a cause for our heedlessness.

When the road becomes long for us, do not let us forget its end.

And when the day of departure comes, make us among those who left this world with tranquil hearts, bearing witness deeds, and faces turned toward Your mercy.

O Allah, place this world in our hands and not in our hearts, make the best of our deeds our last ones, and the best of our days the day we meet You.

Take shade for a moment… then move on.

For the wise person does not ask the tree:

How long will I remain under your shade?

Rather, he asks himself:

Have I taken from this shade what will assist me to complete the journey?

And that is the whole of this world…

A passing shadow, an extended path, and an appointment that will not be delayed.
