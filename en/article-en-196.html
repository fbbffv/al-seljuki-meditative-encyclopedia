Processions of Martyrdom and Faces of Tyranny: Readings in the Jurisprudence of the Ditch and Eternal Battle

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
{Cursed were the companions of the trench * Of the fire full of fuel * When they were sitting near it * And they, to what they were doing against the believers, were witnesses}
[Surah Al-Buruj: 4-7]

In humanity's long procession extending across the centuries, scenes resemble one another and features match, even if names change and eras turn. It is the eternal, ultimate battle—the decisive battle between brutal, grim tyranny that reverts to the abyss of mud and arrogance, and high, free faith that flutters in the horizons of light and liberty. It is a battle never measured by the small standards of the earth, nor weighed in the heavy, losing balance of matter; rather, it is weighed in the eternal balance of Heaven, where souls attach themselves to their Creator and break free from the constraints of matter and the gravitation of mortality, ascending in the ladder of certainty rejoicing, while tyrants plummet into the lowest depth of humiliation and curse, even if they owned the thrones of the earth, possessed the treasuries of matter, and surrounded themselves with walls of iron and fire.

It is that crushing cry, roaring like a waterfall, which shakes the thrones of falsehood and uproots the foundations of injustice in every time and place: "Qutila" (Cursed/Destroyed)... a single word summarizing Heaven's stern decree: meaning cursed, destroyed, and expelled from the mercy of Allah is everyone who wrote with his hand a plan of torture, or raised a whip in the face of a person of faith, or thought that material power is capable of vanquishing the human spirit connected to the Highest Realm.

Destroyed are the owners of dark prisons; those who delusionally reckon that they lock up breaths and shackle thoughts behind deaf walls and rusty bars. Little did they know—in the surge of their arrogance—that hearts which have tasted the sweetness of faith fly in the expanses of the seven heavens, crossing exile and borders, while they—the executioners—are the truly imprisoned ones in the jail of their confusion, the servitude of their desires, and their chronic fear of the coming dawn of truth.

And destroyed are the perpetrators of torture; whose hearts have been covered in harshness, so they revel in human pain, delight in groans of agony, and watch victims with a stony apathy that exactly resembles the apathy of the first companions of the ditch as they sat on the edge of the pit, witnessing the burning of human flesh without shedding a single tear or feeling a single pulse of humanity in their chests.

And destroyed are the perpetrators of displacement; who drive the innocent from their homes, exile women and children, and leave people wandering in open deserts, to satisfy an illusory, crumbling pride and a false assumption that the earth belongs to their whims and caprices. It escaped them that the earth belongs to Allah, who grants it as an inheritance to His righteous servants, and that the oppressed exile wandering in Allah's land is the victorious stranger, while the tyrant settled upon his throne is the outcast stranger expelled from the mercy of the Lord of the worlds.

Look at long history and contemporary reality; you will find them a continuous reel of recurring ditches that never end. Yesterday it was the deep trench of Najran, and today we see various ditches dug in every corner and land: deep prisons, terrible detention camps, distant exiles, and camps gnawed by cold and hunger. The fuel changes in every age; sometimes it is wood and burning logs, sometimes it is whips lashing backs, electric shocks cutting off breaths, solitary cells killing the soul slowly, and at times it is destructive bombing with iron and fire, suffocating sieges, and organized starvation.

Yet the tyrant—in all these forms—is the tyrant: a terrified creature, shaken by the truthful word of faith, and frightened by the firm eye that does not break before his arrogance. The tyrant in his reality is weaker than a butterfly; he sees in the victim's steadfastness the downfall of his kingdom, and sees in the martyr's smile the shattering of his prestige. Thus, he finds no alternative but digging the ditch and igniting the fire to hide his shame in it, and to cover with it the terror of his walls and his crumbling soul that is gnawed by the fear of the unknown.

However, the real scale—the pure balance of faith—shines in its clarity and brilliance through the reality of inevitable cosmic departure. The owner of the ditch will depart; yes, this inflated human idol will collapse and drag into the dust, humbled and lowly, just as the one thrown into the ditch departed. Both bodies leave this narrow earth; this one's body burned in the fire and his pure soul departed to its Creator, satisfied, pleasing, and smiling, while that one died on his velvet bed or in his fortified castle, surrounded by curses, suffocated by terror, and haunted by the blood of victims.

And before Allah, opponents will assemble! There, in the arena of absolute truth and the supreme divine court, where feeble thrones fall, armies and weapons vanish, and false titles and medals evaporate, the executioner and the victim stand face to face. There are no whips there, no bars, no guards protecting the throne, and no henchmen executing orders. The tyrant stands stripped of all power, shackled by his crimes, and the believer stands free, crowned with his patience and steadfastness.

It is as though this stern divine judgment, this decisive divine decree pronounced by the Quran: {Cursed were the companions of the trench}, is a verdict extracted from the justice of the Hereafter, uttered by revelation and declared by cosmic sunnah ahead of its time in this fleeting world. It sounds forth to be a balm and consolation for the afflicted and oppressed, and a terrifying warning that drives sleep away from the eyelids of criminals. It is the declaration announcing in plain daylight: Real death is not the departure of the soul nor the burning of the body; rather, real death is the death of conscience, relapse into the abyss of human downfall, and living under the boots of tyrants. The owner of the ditch was killed morally, psychologically, and historically, falling from the ledger of humanity before dying physically, while the blood of martyrs remained lanterns of light and radiance, illuminating the path for those walking in the footsteps of the prophets and messengers.

It is a war of symbols deeply embedded in human consciousness: the symbol of the whip and executioner that twists like a venomous snake, yet breaks and shatters against the rock of certainty and steadfastness of faith; the symbol of heavy iron chains that rust and corrode with time, while the believer's spirit flies like a soaring falcon in the sky, free and uncontainable; the symbol of the shaking, trembling throne that needs every day mounds of innocent skulls and oceans of victims' blood to remain stable for a few more days; and the symbol of pure prostration to Allah, Lord of the worlds, which firms feet in times of earthquakes and raises foreheads high and proud to the heavens. It is the conflict of darkness and light, mud and spirit, baseness and sublimity, submission and freedom.

The caravan of faith and martyrdom will continue to march on its accustomed path, with banners of true victory waving on the horizon—the victory of faith over pain, the victory of eternal principle over cheap compromise, and the victory of the transcendent spirit over fleeting matter. And tyrants will remain—blindly wandering in their error—digging their ditches and marching to their doom, oblivious to a grand truth: every ditch they dig to torture the free is but a real grave in which they bury their thrones and imagined prestige, and an eternal historical testament proving the immortality of the victim and the destruction of the executioner.
