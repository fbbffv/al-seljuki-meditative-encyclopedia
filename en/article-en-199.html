Passengers on the Train of Time…
Contemplating the Counsel "As if You Were a Stranger"

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a traveler along a path."
[Narrated by Al-Bukhari]

In the bustle of life, amid the clamor of markets, the noise of cities, and the acceleration of hours, a person sometimes moves forward as though created to remain forever, as if this world were his eternal home, and as if the doors of departure would never open for him!

He builds…

And amasses…

And plans…

And contends…

And grows angry…

And grieves over what he missed…

And rejoices in what he possessed…

Then he forgets the one truth no two people disagree upon:

He is a traveler.

And the train is carrying him forward, whether he pays attention to its movement or remains heedless of it.

Every sunrise is a station…

Every sunset is a station…

Every passing year is another carriage of life's train…

And every breath that leaves and returns is new testimony that the journey has not stopped.

Then comes a day when the train has no further station in this world…

And the final departure is announced.

You Are in This World… But You Are Not of It

How deep are these prophetic words!

"Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a traveler along a path."

He ﷺ did not say: Abandon the world.

Nor did he say: Forsake people.

Nor did he say: Do not work, do not marry, do not build, and do not seek sustenance.

Rather, he taught you something far finer and greater:

Live in this world… but do not let the world live in your heart.

Be in it with your body, but keep your heart attached to that which is with Allah.

Work in it, but do not worship it.

Possess from it, but do not let it possess you.

Enjoy its blessings, but do not forget the Bestower of blessings.

Build in it, but remember that you will depart from it.

For the stranger knows that he is a stranger.

And the traveler knows that he is a traveler.

As for the heedless one, he builds palaces along the road, then grows furious when told:

It is time to depart.

Lighten Your Luggage… For the Journey Is Short

Consider the traveler as he prepares to depart.

He does not carry his entire house with him.

Nor does he place everything he owns into his bag.

Rather, he chooses what he needs and leaves behind what burdens him.

And so should the heart be.

For how many a person carries baggage in his chest heavier than mountains!

Rancor…

Envy…

Enmity…

Jealousy…

A desire for revenge…

An old sorrow…

A word he never forgave…

A situation he never forgot…

Then he moves through life heavily burdened!

O traveler of life…

Lighten your load.

Forgive whomever you can.

Pardon the one who wronged you.

And leave behind what does not deserve to reside in your heart.

For perhaps a day will come when you wish to carry to your grave a light heart, not a chest filled with rivalries.

The Passageway Is Not the Abode

This is the entire issue:

This world is a passageway… not a permanent abode.

This world is an abode of action… and the Hereafter is the abode of recompense.

This world is an abode of planting… and the Hereafter is the abode of harvest.

This world is a journey… and the Hereafter is the arrival.

How can a traveler settle permanently in a station he knows he will leave?

And how can a person make dust a palace for his heart, knowing he will one day be carried beneath the dust?

There is no harm in having a beautiful house.

There is no harm in having wealth.

There is no harm in succeeding in your work.

There is no harm in rejoicing in the blessings of Allah.

But the danger lies in all of that becoming the ultimate goal of the heart.

When money becomes a master, position an object of devotion, praise a form of sustenance, appearances an identity, people a measure, and this world the ultimate wish!

As for the believer, he says:

Everything I have is a trust, everything in my hand is borrowed, and everything I love in this world is subject to parting.

Asceticism Is Not Leaving the World… But Not Letting the World Leave Allah Out of Your Heart

A man may possess the world while his heart is poor before Allah.

And another may possess but little while his heart is the richest of people through Allah.

For asceticism is not a coarse garment, nor an empty house, nor the abandonment of blessings.

Asceticism is for the world to be in your hand, not in your heart.

If it comes, you give thanks.

If it leaves, you remain patient.

If Allah gives you, you do not transgress.

If He withhold from you, you do not grow discontent.

If you rise, you do not grow arrogant.

If you fall, you do not break.

Because your heart was never attached to the station; rather, it was attached to the One in Whose Hand is the entire journey.

"When morning comes, do not expect evening, and when evening comes, do not expect morning. Take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death."

"When Morning Comes, Do Not Expect Evening"

Among the completions of this great advice is what the Prophet ﷺ said to Ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both):

"When morning comes, do not expect evening, and when evening comes, do not expect morning. Take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death."

What words to awaken the heedless!

Do not say:

I will repent tomorrow.

I will give charity tomorrow.

I will rectify my relationship with Allah tomorrow.

I will apologize tomorrow.

I will be dutiful to my parents tomorrow.

I will memorize the Quran tomorrow.

I will leave sin tomorrow.

And who informed you that you have a tomorrow?

How many a person slept with many plans in his heart, only to wake up with the entire file of this world closed!

How many a person said: "When I grow older, I will do…" yet never reached old age.

And how many a person said: "When I have free time…" then that which leaves a person no free time thereafter came upon him.

Tomorrow is not yours.

What is yours is now.

So seize it.

Do Not Ask How Long You Lived… But What You Planted

When the journey ends, the question will not be:

How many houses did you build?

Nor:

How many cars did you own?

Nor:

How much wealth did you collect?

Nor:

How many people knew your name?

Rather, the ultimate truth will be:

What did you present?

What did you plant along the road?

Whose heart did you comfort?

Whom did you teach?

Whom did you help?

For whom did you pray?

What charity left a lasting impact?

What knowledge benefited people?

What kind word remained in a person's heart after your departure?

People may forget your house…

But they may never forget that you opened a door for them when doors were closed.

They may forget your wealth…

But they may remember that you held their hand during their hardest days.

They may forget your name…

But Allah does not forget your impact.

When You Understand That You Are a Traveler… The World Shrinks in Your Eyes

Then not every word deserves your anger.

Nor every offense deserves a war.

Nor every loss deserves your collapse.

Nor every success deserves your vanity.

Nor every praise deserves your exultation.

Nor every criticism deserves your grief.

Because you know that you are a traveler along a path.

And a traveler does not build his entire life upon an incident that occurred at a station.

If the train is delayed… he remains patient.

If the route changes… he seeks reward with Allah.

If he loses something… he compensates for it as best he can.

If he bids someone farewell… he prays for him.

Then he continues marching forward.

Such is the believer.

When a calamity befalls him, he says:

Indeed, to Allah we belong, and to Him we shall return.

When a blessing reaches him, he says:

Praise be to Allah.

When loss comes, he remains patient.

When giving comes, he expresses gratitude.

Because he knows that the journey is not entirely according to his desires, but it is all within the knowledge and wisdom of Allah.

Do Not Attach Yourself to the Station… For the Call Is Coming

O traveler…

Reflect upon the faces of those who were around you years ago.

Where are some of them?

They departed.

Where are the houses that were filled with life?

They changed.

Where are the lives that were once strong?

They aged.

Where are the days you thought would never end?

They ended.

And today you stand at another station.

You think you are stationary…

While the truth is that you are moving.

Time is the train that never stops.

Every minute tells you:

You have drawn closer.

Every sunrise says:

You have decreased from the journey by a day.

Every sunset says:

A page has been closed, never to return.

What heedlessness it is to see the train of life passing before our eyes, yet we treat it as though the journey were eternal!

The True Homeland Awaits

The true homeland is not here.

Not in a house, no matter how beautiful.

Nor in a land, no matter how beloved to us.

Nor in wealth, no matter how abundant.

Nor in a position, no matter how high.

The true homeland is where there is no death.

No parting.

No illness.

No old age.

No fear.

No sorrow.

Paradise.

There, the feeling of being a stranger ends.

There, the traveler rests.

There, a person no longer needs a travel bag.

There, there is no final station followed by another.

There is eternity.

And there lies the greatest victory for man: to arrive with a sound heart, a clear record, and righteous deeds.

Conclusion: We Are Passersby… So Let Our Passing Be Beautiful

O son of Adam…

You are a traveler.

So do not turn the journey into a long feud.

Nor turn the road into envy.

Nor turn the stations into objects of worship.

And carry with you to the end nothing except that which benefits you.

Pray.

Work.

Give charity.

Forgive.

Excel in goodness.

Learn.

Leave an impact.

And repent to Allah before the journey ends.

Make every morning a new beginning.

Every evening a pause for self-reckoning.

Every calamity a reminder.

Every blessing a cause for gratitude.

And every day a step toward Allah.

For this world is not an abiding home…

It is merely a transient platform on the train of time.

So do not busy yourself too much with decorating the platform while forgetting that the train will depart.

Do not busy yourself with gathering the bags of this world while forgetting the provisions for the Hereafter.

And do not let your heart become a prisoner to a station you know with certainty you will leave.

Be in this world as if you were a stranger… or a traveler along a path.

How beautiful it is for the traveler to reach the end of the road having left good behind him, mercy before him, certainty in his heart, and light upon his record.

O Allah, make our journey in this world a journey of obedience, make our departure a departure of acceptance, and make our final abode the highest Paradise of Al-Firdaus.
