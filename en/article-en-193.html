When Scales Are Inverted… and the Base Person Becomes the Happiest of People in the World!

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
"The Hour will not be established until the happiest of people in the world is Luk'a, son of Luk'a (a base, foolish person, son of a base person)."
[Narrated by Al-Tirmidhi]

In this majestic prophetic Hadith, there is a scene from the scenes of the end of time. The intention behind it is not merely to report about specific individuals, nor to describe a passing social state; rather, it is a disclosure of a dangerous disease that afflicts the balance of life when value is separated from appearance, dignity from position, virtue from money, and humanity from power.

It is a moment when the question: "Who are you?" becomes less important than the question: "What do you own? Whom do you know? And how much can you impose yourself?"

The person of chivalry becomes a stranger, the person of good character becomes naive in the eyes of people, and the trustworthy person becomes unable to compete with the deceitful, while the voice of the mean person rises; because he has mastered the language of interests, and learned how to sell principles in a market that does not ask about the price.

Here comes the Hadith as a cry that shakes the heart:

"Until the happiest of people in the world is Luk'a, son of Luk'a."

Meaning: the inversion of standards reaches a point where he who is characterized by meanness, baseness, weak chivalry, foolishness, or bad character becomes among the most fortunate people in the enjoyment and adornment of the world.

This is not a call to despise people because of their lineages or origins, for Islam did not place dignity in tribe, money, color, or prestige; rather, it made the true scale piety and righteous deeds.

Allah Almighty said:

"Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you." [Al-Hujurat: 13]

Here the great paradox becomes clear:

A person may be grand in the eyes of people, while he is small in the scale of Allah.

And he may be unknown on earth, while he is known in heaven.

A man may walk in the markets without anyone turning to him, yet with Allah he holds a station that owners of palaces cannot reach.

And another may sit on a high chair, surrounded by lights, entourage, and applause, yet his heart is empty of piety, his tongue knows nothing but lies, his hand extends only to self-interest, and his soul moves only behind the desire for power and money.

So which of them is happier?

It is not a matter of a chair or a palace, but rather a matter of a scale.

When the Scale Dies

Societies do not collapse suddenly.

They begin when a person’s definition of success changes.

When the noble person becomes the one who gathered money, not the one who preserved trust.

And the strong person becomes the one who can silence others, not the one who can control himself.

And the clever person becomes the one who knows how to deceive, not the one who knows how to be truthful.

And the successful person becomes the one who climbed on the shoulders of people, not the one who carried some of the burdens of life for them.

And the failure becomes the person of principle; because he refused to sell his conscience.

Here the tragedy begins.

For corruption does not always start from laws, but rather starts from changing concepts.

When the liar is applauded because he is successful, the truthful person is despised because he did not gather money, the door is opened to the hypocrite because he is good at flattery, and it is closed in the face of the trustworthy because he is not good at bowing; then society has begun manufacturing the "Luk'a" of whom the Hadith spoke.

It is a collective industry sometimes.

For the mean person does not always rise alone; many hands may raise him.

And the hypocrite does not make his glory by himself only; rather, an audience that adores applause makes it for him.

And the corrupt person does not continue without an environment that tolerates him.

And the oppressor’s survival does not last long if he finds before him a nation that fears none but Allah.

Therefore, the inversion of scales is not a matter of a single person, but a matter of a social conscience when it falls ill.

When Money Becomes a Certificate of Virtue

Among the most dangerous illusions of life is for a person to think that an abundance of money is evidence of Allah’s pleasure.

No.

For money may be a blessing, a trial, a gradual enticement, or a test.

Allah Almighty said:

"And let not their wealth and their children impress you. Allah only intends to punish them through them in worldly life." [At-Tawbah: 55]

So money alone does not tell you: its owner is righteous.

And position does not tell you: its owner is generous.

And fame does not tell you: its owner is great.

And a palace does not bear witness for its owner with Allah.

And a luxury car does not raise a person a single degree in the scale of the Hereafter.

And an entourage walking behind a man does not mean that the angels love him.

The world is not a certificate of good conduct!

Allah may give the world to those He loves and those He does not love.

As for faith, guidance, and success, that is another door.

That is why it was among the greatest trials for a person to confuse worldly giving with divine honor.

A Picture from the Reality of Life

Look around you…

You may see a man who knows nothing of religion except its name, yet he leads gatherings.

And you may see a silent, trustworthy man who owns nothing but his salary and his dignity.

You may see a person of knowledge marginalized, and a person of noise elevated.

You may see one who speaks much being heard, and one who knows much not being asked.

You may see one who flatters people of influence becoming close to them, and one who speaks the truth becoming heavy upon them.

And you may see the person of principle paying the price for his principle, while the person of interest reaps the profits of his concessions.

So the soul says: Where is justice?!

"And the Hereafter is better and more enduring."
"And the worldly life is not except enjoyment of delusion."

The world is short, no matter how long it lasts.

And position is transient, no matter how high it rises.

And fame is a shadow, no matter how far it extends.

And money is a number that changes.

And the body you boast of today will return to the dust.

And the tongue with which people praise you today may fall silent tomorrow.

And the question from which there is no escape remains:

What have you done with your humanity? What have you done with your heart? What have you done with your conscience?

The Problem Is Not that the Mean Person Advances… But that Meanness Becomes a Value

There is a difference between a mean person living in the world, and the world turning into an environment that rewards meanness.

The first is a trial.

As for the second, it is an inversion.

That lying becomes a skill.

And deception becomes cleverness.

And hypocrisy becomes politics.

And bribery becomes facilitation.

And oppression becomes firmness.

And cowardice becomes wisdom.

And insolence becomes boldness.

And flattery becomes diplomacy.

And silence regarding the truth becomes "rationality."

Here names are lost before facts are lost.

And when the names of things change, falsehood becomes more dangerous.

Because if a person knows falsehood as falsehood, he fights it.

But if falsehood is called success, he may run after it.

And if hypocrisy is called intelligence, he may learn it.

And if corruption is called shrewdness, he may boast of it.

Thus a society is defeated before its institutions are destroyed; because the true defeat begins in the conscience.

The Believer Does Not Measure Himself by the World’s Market

The believer lives in the world, but he does not make the world his scale.

He works, trades, builds, learns, seeks provision, and strives for strength, but he does not worship results.

If money comes to him, he thanks.

And if it is delayed from him, he is patient.

And if he rises in the world, he does not grow arrogant.

And if he falls, he does not break.

Because he knows that his value is not in his pocket.

Nor in his chair.

Nor in the number of those who applaud him.

Nor in the number of those who fear him.

Rather, it is in his relationship with Allah.

Therefore, Allah Almighty said:

"Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you."

This verse destroys an entire market of illusions.

For no title elevates its owner without piety.

And no money saves him without action.

And no lineage enriches him without faith.

And no authority grants him immortality.

When the Hour Approaches… Truth Is Bare

Among the signs of the end of time is that many standards become disrupted, so people see matters through the eyes of the world.

In that, there may be a severe trial for the believer:

Does he follow people when they applaud?

Or does he follow the truth when everyone falls silent?

Does he make money the criterion for success?

Or does he make Allah’s pleasure his goal?

Does he rejoice if he rises in the eyes of creation?

Or does he ask: How am I with the Creator?

It is an inner battle whose noise no one hears.

A battle between the glitter of the world and the light of faith.

Between saying: "What will I gain?"

And asking: "Does this please Allah?"

Between searching for people’s admiration, and searching for Allah’s acceptance.

Do Not Be Deceived by the Scene

You may see the world smiling at a man who does not deserve it.

Do not envy.

And do not let the scene shatter your certainty.

You may see a person rising unjustly.

Do not think his rise is a testimony for him.

You may see the person of principle tired.

Do not think that Allah has humiliated him.

For the fatigue that preserved his heart may be better than the rest that corrupted the heart of another.

And withholding may be protection.

And delay may be upbringing.

And poverty may be a test.

And wealth may be a trial.

And fame may be a gradual enticement.

And seclusion may be salvation.

And staying away from the spotlight may be closeness to Allah.

So do not read a person's destiny from the page of the world alone; for the book has not ended yet.

And There… Where Every Mask Falls

The day will come when position will not avail.

Nor clan.

Nor money.

Nor an audience.

Nor an entourage.

Nor media.

Nor applause.

Nor closed doors.

Nor open doors.

There, man stands alone.

Allah Almighty said:

"The Day when there will not benefit wealth or children, except one who comes to Allah with a sound heart."

There, the mean person knows that everything he gathered was a mirage.

And the arrogant knows that the chair was not a throne.

And the hypocrite knows that applause was not salvation.

And the oppressed knows that Allah did not forget him.

And the person of piety knows that the years of patience did not go in vain.

Only there… scales become straight.

The Message of the Hadith to Us

The intention of the Hadith is not for us to sit watching people and distributing titles of "mean ones" to them, but to begin with ourselves.

For the most dangerous question is not: Who is Luk'a, son of Luk'a?

Rather: Is there anything of the character of meanness in myself?

Do I sell my principle for a benefit?

Do I flatter at the expense of truth?

Do I rejoice at the fall of others?

Do I despise the poor?

Do I respect the rich because he is rich?

Do I praise the person of power out of fear of him?

Do I stay silent about error because I have an interest?

Do I measure people by what is in their pockets?

Do I make the world a criterion for dignity?

Here true contemplation begins.

For the Quran and the Hadith do not want us to see the flaws of the world only, but to see the flaws of ourselves in the mirror of revelation.

Conclusion: Do Not Be Great in a Small Market

O Son of Adam…

Do not be deceived by a city that applauds you, if Allah is not pleased with you.

And do not be saddened by a path that was not opened for you, if Allah has protected you from a trial you do not know.

And do not think that the owner of money is happier than you.

Nor that the owner of position is higher than you.

Nor that the owner of fame is more successful than you.

If your heart knows Allah, your tongue is truthful, your hand gives, your soul is chaste, and your conscience awakens if you err; then you have been given of the world what is more precious than much of what competitors compete for.

The greatest victory in a time of inverted scales is to remain upright when the road deviates.

And to remain truthful when lying becomes trade.

And to remain trustworthy when betrayal becomes cleverness.

And to remain chaste when vulgarity becomes fame.

And to remain humble when arrogance becomes a commodity.

And to choose Allah when the world gives you a choice between Allah and the world.

For the world, no matter how vast, is narrow.

And life, no matter how long, is short.

And authority, no matter how grand, is vanishing.

And money, no matter how abundant, is perishable.

As for what is with Allah… it remains.

"And Allah is better and more enduring."

So if you see that scales have become disrupted, do not be disrupted with them.

And if you see that the mean person has risen, do not make his height a criterion for your insignificance.

And if you see that falsehood owns the platforms, do not think that truth has died.

For falsehood may rise for an hour…

And the mean person may rise for a time…

But Allah does not leave the scale of truth broken forever.

And life in its entirety is a short phase between a beginning we do not choose and an end we cannot delay.

So choose for yourself a place that does not vanish:

In the mercy of Allah, not in the memory of people.

And in the scale of Allah, not in the market of the world.

And with the truthful, not with the applauders.
