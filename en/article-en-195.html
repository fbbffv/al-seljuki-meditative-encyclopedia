“And You Will Return to Where You Began…” When Days Turn and Islam Becomes a Stranger Again

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
“Iraq withheld its dirham and its qafiz, Sham withheld its mudi and its dinar, and Egypt withheld its irdabb and its dinar, and you will return to where you began, and you will return to where you began, and you will return to where you began.”
[Narrated by Muslim]

This Sentence Is Not Mere Passing News… It Is a Bell That Shakes the Heart

There are words in the Prophetic Sunnah that pass over the ears as if they were few, but when the heart stops before them for a long time, it finds behind them vast horizons of lessons, divine laws, and destinies.

Among these words are:

“And you will return to where you began.”

Three times…

Three consecutive knocks on the door of heedlessness!

It is as if the Prophet ﷺ did not want this sentence to pass by quickly; rather, he wanted it to settle in the memory, to take root in the conscience, and for the Muslim to know that history is not a straight line that constantly ascends forward. Nations may experience the turning of days, returning to a point they thought they had transcended forever.

How many a nation thought it had reached the peak… then fell!

How many a civilization thought its sun would never set… then it set!

How many a people ruled the earth… then became mere memories of the past!

And how many a banner was raised until it filled the horizon… then nothing remained of it except a mention in history books!

It is the divine law of Allah regarding nations:

“And these days We alternate among the people.” [Al 'Imran: 140]

Where Did Islam Begin? And Where Can the Ummah Return?

Islam began as a stranger…

There was no great state around the Prophet ﷺ, no massive armies, no full treasuries, and no towering palaces.

There was one man believing in Allah.

Then a woman.

Then a youth.

Then men and women who believed in this light.

And almost all of Mecca was on the other side.

In its beginning, Islam was a faint voice in human calculations, but it carried the power of heaven.

It did not have numbers, but it had the truth.

It did not have earthly authority, but it had the authority of faith.

It did not have the adornments of the world, but it had the revelation of Allah.

Then the light spread from Mecca to Medina, from Medina to the Peninsula, and then crossed oceans, mountains, and deserts, until Islam became a great civilization, and its state extended east and west.

Yet the Quran did not teach Muslims that when power comes, it becomes eternal.

Rather, it taught them that power itself is a test.

Sovereignty itself is a test.

Civilization itself is a test.

And victory itself is a test.

“Islam Began as Something Strange and It Will Return to Being Strange”

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

“Indeed, Islam began as something strange and it will return to being strange as it began, so glad tidings to the strangers.”

So estrangement is not necessarily the estrangement of place.

A person may live among millions of Muslims, yet be a stranger to the reality of Islam.

He may live in a society that raises a thousand religious slogans, yet the values of Islam in it are strange.

A Muslim may be a stranger because he refuses to sell his religion for self-interest.

A stranger because he does not lie when people lie.

A stranger because he does not oppress when he is capable of oppression.

A stranger because he does not consume the forbidden when the forbidden becomes familiar.

A stranger because he does not make religion subservient to desire, nor the truth subservient to interest.

A stranger because he says: “Allah said,” when people say: “The market said.”

And he says: “The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said,” when people say: “The group said.”

And he says: “This is truth,” when people say: “But everyone is doing it!”

That is the estrangement that requires patience.

Iraq, Sham, and Egypt… The Geography of the Hadith and the Memory of Civilization

It is not surprising that the Hadith mentions Iraq, Sham, and Egypt.

These lands are not merely spots on a map.

They are massive pages from the history of humanity.

Iraq… the land of the two rivers, the land of the Tigris and Euphrates, the land of Babylon and Baghdad, the land of scholars, academies, jurists, and Hadith scholars.

Sham… the land of prophets, Damascus, Jerusalem, and the land of conquests, knowledge, and steadfastness.

And Egypt… the land of the Nile, Al-Azhar, sciences, and its location that made it through the centuries a beating heart in the region.

Then comes the Hadith to describe the withholding of blessings, wealth, and measures.

Here a deep image emerges:

The land that was once a source of goodness, power, and wealth may go through phases of turmoil, withholding, siege, and political and economic shifts.

The balances of power may change.

Wealth may transfer.

And people may lose the blessings of their lands due to trials, wars, and conflicts.

All of this reminds man that wealth is not the true master of history.

How Balances Change!

At one stage in history, the world looked at Baghdad in awe.

Then came days when Baghdad was no longer as it used to be.

Damascus was a center among centers of civilization.

Then successive trials, wars, and transformations befell it.

Egypt was among the greatest centers of knowledge, politics, and economy.

Then eras changed and conditions shifted.

And here is history repeating its lessons:

• Power does not last.

• States do not endure forever.

• Wealth does not protect its owner from divine laws.

• Civilization cannot live without values.

• And an Ummah that loses its spirit will not be saved by the abundance of its buildings.

Buildings may rise to the sky, while morals descend to the ground.

Markets may become crowded, while hearts become empty.

Devices may proliferate, while awareness diminishes.

The world may become connected via the internet, while man becomes disconnected from his Lord.

Here lies the real question: Have we advanced in means only, or have we advanced in the human being?

The Return Is Not Necessarily the Return of Place… But the Return of Condition

This is a point where we ought to pause for a long time.

The saying of the Prophet ﷺ: “And you will return to where you began,” should not be loaded beyond what it can bear, nor should we make every political or economic event in our era a definitive application of the Hadith.

For the Hadith is from the news of the unseen that we believe in; as for assigning a specific contemporary event as being definitively intended, it requires knowledge and evidence, not mere resemblance.

However, the great meaning that awakens the heart is clear:

The Ummah may pass through phases in which some manifestations of estrangement that Islam knew in its beginning return.

A Muslim may return to feeling that he carries the truth in an environment that does not honor it.

Uprightness may become arduous.

Steadfastness upon religion may become costly.

Truthfulness may become strange.

Chastity may become strange.

Trustworthiness may become strange.

And holding fast to the truth may become strange.

Here the value of: “So glad tidings to the strangers” becomes clear.

“Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you.”
[Al-Hujurat: 13]

The Most Dangerous Estrangement Is Not the Muslim’s Estrangement from People… But Islam’s Estrangement in the Muslim’s Heart

A person may live in an Islamic country, pray, fast, and raise the slogan of religion, yet live by a logic far removed from the spirit of Islam.

He oppresses the worker. Eats the right of the weak. Lies in trade. Cheats in weight. Despises the poor. Oppresses women. And cuts ties of kinship.

Then he says: I am a Muslim.

But Islam is not merely a word spoken.

Islam is trustworthiness. Islam is justice. Islam is mercy. Islam is truthfulness. Islam is worship. Islam is morality.

Islam is for man to be a servant to Allah in secret just as he is a servant to Allah in public.

For the scale of heaven is not the scale of fame, nor wealth, nor tribe, nor position, nor number of followers, nor strength of connections. It is piety.

When Things Advance and Man Recedes

We live in an era astonishing in its ability to manufacture things.

An airplane flies thousands of kilometers. A phone carries the entire world in the palm of the hand. Artificial intelligence writes, draws, and analyzes. Medicine advances. Sciences expand. Cities rise.

Yet… Has man become more merciful? Has he become more truthful? Has he become more just? Has he become more fearful of Allah?

Here the Quran stands before modern civilization with the same question it confronted ancient nations with: What have you done with man?

For man can possess technology, then use it in oppression.

He can possess wealth, then buy consciences with it.

He can possess media, then manufacture lies with it.

He can possess power, then enslave the weak with it.

He can possess knowledge, then use it against the truth.

The problem is not always the lack of knowledge. Sometimes the problem is the separation of knowledge from piety.

History Is a Witness That Complements No One

Read history…

You will see empires rise and then fall.

You will see kings who filled the earth with noise, then found nothing in their graves except their deeds.

You will see palaces that were bustling with guards and servants, then became ruins inhabited by winds.

You will see cities that were capitals of the world, then a traveler passes by them not knowing they were once centers of civilization.

These are not stories for entertainment. These are messages.

Every stone in history says: Do not be deceived.

Every grave says: You will end.

Every collapsed civilization says: Nothing in the world is immune to the law of Allah.

And every rise carries within it a test of falling.

The Most Dangerous Thing in the Hadith Is Not Fear of the Future… But the Responsibility of the Present

The intention is not for us to sit watching events and saying: “Here is the prophecy being fulfilled!” and then walk away. No.

The Prophetic Sunnah does not build in the believer a spectator mindset; rather, it builds a prepared mindset.

If the time of estrangement is coming or has arrived in some of its forms, what is required?

To hold fast to Allah. To rectify your home. To teach your children. To guard your tongue. To fulfill trusts. To abandon oppression.

Not to let trial become greater than your religion. Not to let politics corrupt your morals. Not to let dispute make lying permissible for you.

Not to make disagreement with a person a reason to oppress him. To remain a Muslim human being when many people around you collapse.

For steadfastness does not begin from the street… it begins from the heart.

O Son of Adam… Do Not Wait for the World to Be Rectified for You to Be Rectified

A person might say: “How can I be righteous while people are corrupt?” This is a wrong question.

Noah (peace be upon him) did not wait for his people to become righteous to obey his Lord.

Abraham (peace be upon him) did not wait for his people’s approval to declare Monotheism.

Moses (peace be upon him) did not wait for Pharaoh’s calm to speak the truth.

And the Prophet ﷺ did not wait for Mecca to become believing to begin the call.

Truth does not derive its value from the number of its followers. And falsehood does not become truth because millions do it. Truth is truth even if you are alone.

However, that does not mean that everyone who opposes people is upon the truth; the scale is the Book of Allah, the Sunnah of His Messenger ﷺ, knowledge, and justice—not merely the feeling of estrangement.

It Is Man’s Battle with Himself Before It Is with the World

When the world becomes turbulent, the metal of the heart appears.

In ease, everyone may seem steadfast. But during trial… the truthful appears. The patient appears. The person of principle appears. And the one whose religion was a mere habit appears.

Allah Almighty said: “Do the people think that they will be left to say, ‘We believe’ and they will not be tried?” [Al-Ankabut: 2]

Trial is not always a punishment. It may be a test. It may be revealing. It may be a sieve. Dropping masks. Revealing hearts. And showing who was worshipping Allah, and who was worshipping his image before people.

“The Flesh and Blood of Abu Hurairah Bore Witness to That”

Then comes the statement of Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): “The flesh and blood of Abu Hurairah bore witness to that.”

What a statement! It is not merely a piece of information passed by a narrator. It is the testimony of a man who lived with prophethood.

A man who heard the news from the mouth of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, carried it in his chest, and then said: My flesh and blood bore witness to that. Meaning: I bear responsibility for what I transmit. I heard it. I memorized it. And I conveyed it.

This reminds us of the trust in transmitting the Sunnah. Not every phrase attributed to the Prophet ﷺ is a Hadith. Not every religious story is true. And not every prophecy published on social media is authentic. In an era of rapid publication, scientific integrity becomes worship.

A Time of Trials… When the Word Becomes Faster Than a Bullet

In the past, lying required time to spread. Today, a single click may suffice.

An image without a source. News without verification. A fabricated Hadith. A severed interpretation. A clipped video snippet. Then a person says: “Share and be rewarded!” While what he published might cause the misguidance of thousands of people.

Therefore, among the greatest forms of piety in this era is: To pause before you publish. Ask: Is it proven? Is the source trustworthy? Did I understand the text? Did I convey the words of scholars with integrity? Did I put the Hadith in its context? Did I turn speculation into certainty?

The Muslim in times of trials does not need only a righteous heart… he also needs a verifying mind.

Salvation Is Not in Knowing the Date of the End… But in Preparing to Meet Allah

People may become busy asking: When will it occur? When will this appear? Is this nation the intended one? Is this war the sign? Has the Hour drawn near?

Yet the greater question is: What have you prepared?

For a person may not reach the end of time, but he will reach the end of his life. He may not witness the standing of the Hour, but he will witness the standing of his own hour: the day he dies.

Here all analyses end. Politics ends. Wealth ends. Fame ends. Disputes end. And man remains alone before Allah.

Allah Almighty said: “And all of them are coming to Him on the Day of Resurrection alone.” [Maryam: 95]

What a word that destroys all human arrogance: alone. No army. No position. No tribe. No wealth. No followers. Only: you… your deed… and your Lord.

If Estrangement Returns… Be Among the Strangers to Whom Glad Tidings Belong

If trustworthiness becomes strange… be trustworthy.

If truthfulness becomes strange… be truthful.

If justice becomes strange… be just.

If forgiveness becomes strange… forgive.

If prayer becomes heavy upon people… do not abandon it.

If the Quran becomes forsaken… make a place for it in your home.

If the permissible becomes harder… be patient upon it.

If the forbidden becomes familiar… stay away from it.

If people mock your uprightness… remember that people mocked Noah (peace be upon him).

And if you feel that you are alone… remember that Allah does not leave His servant.

Estrangement among people may be an honor with Allah, if it is an estrangement in the truth, not an estrangement from the truth.

Conclusion: Do Not Fear the Turning of Days… Fear for Your Heart

The saying of the Prophet ﷺ: “And you will return to where you began,” is not a call to despair. Nor is it a declaration that history has ended. Nor is it permission for a Muslim to flee from his reality.

It is a wake-up bell. It reminds us that days alternate, nations change, power does not last, estrangement may return, and man should not be deceived by what is in his hand.

So if days turn… do not let your heart turn with them. If people become turbulent… stand firm. If voices mix… return to the Quran and Sunnah. If trials multiply… hold fast to Allah. If the truth becomes strange… do not feel lonely. And if you see the world turning upside down… know that the One Who does not change is Allah.

Maps may change… and Allah remains. States may fall… and Allah remains. Balances may shift… and Allah remains. A generation may pass and a generation may come… and Allah remains.

And people may return to where they began… but the door of repentance remains open as long as the soul has not reached the throat.

So let not your concern be to know where the hands of history have reached… rather make your concern to know: Where has your heart reached with Allah?

Do not only ask: Has the end drawn near? Rather ask: Have I drawn near to my Lord?

For if the Hour is tomorrow, you are accountable. If it is after a thousand years, you are accountable. And if you live until the end of the world, you are accountable. Because the grand end may be far in the calculation of time… but the end of man is closer to him than everything.

So O servant of Allah… hold fast to your religion. Cling to the Book of your Lord. Remember Him much. Supplicate to Him much. Be just. Be merciful. Guard yourself from trials. Do not be a prisoner to every news. Do not believe everything you hear. Do not transmit everything that reaches you. And rectify what is between you and Allah.

So if estrangement returns… glad tidings to a heart that did not change.

If days turn… glad tidings to a servant whose desires did not turn him.

If faces shift… glad tidings to him who stood firm in his direction.

And if the lights of the world go out… glad tidings to him who had in his heart a light from Allah.

O Allah, Turner of the hearts, keep our hearts firm upon Your religion; O Director of hearts, direct our hearts to Your obedience. O Allah, distance us from trials, both apparent and hidden, and grant us insight in religion, steadfastness during trials, truthfulness in speech and deed, a good end, and gather us with Your Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the company of the righteous.

O Allah, do not make us among those who return to where they began in heedlessness; rather, make us among those who began with faith and sealed with faith, began with Monotheism and sealed with Monotheism, and began with the love of Your Messenger ﷺ and sealed upon it.

O Allah, seal for us with goodness, and let not our last words from this world be other than the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.
