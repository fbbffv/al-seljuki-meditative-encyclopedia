When Mercy Overcame Anger
Reflections on the statement of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: "Verily, My Mercy Overcame My Anger"

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
On the authority of Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him), the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"When Allah decreed the creation, He wrote in His Book, which is with Him above the Throne: 'Verily, My mercy overcame My anger.'"
And in another narration: "Verily, My mercy preceded My anger."

A Word Written Above the Throne

There are words that pass through the ears, and then fade away...

And there are words that, once they enter the heart, transform one's entire outlook on life.

This Hadith is among those words that ought not to be read by the eyes alone, but with the heart.

Imagine this magnificent scene...

Allah created all creation, ordained their decrees, and knew what would originate from them and what would befall them; then, as stated in the Hadith, this solemn word was inscribed:

﴿Verily, My mercy overcame My anger﴾

This word is not written in the margins of life.

Nor is it a human promise subject to alteration.

It is a divine word tied to the Unseen, transmitted in authentic Hadith. Its meaning signifies that the mercy of Allah is broader, earlier, and overwhelmingly greater in its manifestations than His wrath and punishment. Hadith commentators have highlighted that the intent is to elucidate the vastness of divine mercy and the multitude of its manifestations across creation.

Here, the heart stands still for a long time...

My mercy overcame My anger.

What a vast door of hope this word has opened for mankind!

Why did He say: "Overcame"?

This single word is an ocean of profound meanings.

The Prophet ﷺ said in one narration: "preceded", and in another: "overcame".

Precedence draws a vivid picture of mercy striding ahead.

As for overcoming, it portrays mercy as overwhelmingly stronger in its effects and broader in its domain.

For man is born into mercy...

• He is born weak, yet encompassed by tender care.

• He is sustained without any power or strength of his own.

• His body grows while he is unaware.

• His heart beats without his command.

• He opens his eyes to a world he did not create.

• The sun rises upon him.

• The night comes for him to rest.

• Water descends from the sky.

• Plants sprout from the earth.

• Birds receive their provision.

• And the bee finds its way to its sustenance.

And even when he disobeys...

Air is not cut off from him.

Sunlight is not withheld from him.

His heart does not stop beating at the first sin.

Nor is it said to him: "You have sinned, so you are cast out of Allah's mercy."

Rather, he is given respite...

He is shielded...

He is reminded...

A sign comes to him...

An admonition knocks on his heart...

He hears a word...

He reads a Hadith...

A moment of regret overtakes him...

Then the door is opened wide before him:

Repent.

Return.

Seek forgiveness.

Start anew.

This is the meaning of victory and dominance in the heart of the believer: that the manifestations of mercy in this world are infinitely greater and broader than the traces of punishment.

Why "Wrote" instead of merely saying: "Decreed"?

Here lies a deep contemplation.

The Hadith did not merely say: Allah decreed that My mercy overcame My anger; rather it explicitly stated:

"He wrote in His Book."

In human linguistic usage, writing carries the connotation of confirmation, documentation, manifestation, and firmness.

When a human writes a promise, he feels that the matter is no longer a fleeting thought.

When he writes a covenant, the covenant becomes ever-present before his eyes.

When a word is recorded in a document, it becomes more enduring than a passing phrase.

How then, when writing is attributed to the Lord of the worlds?!

It is not a writing born out of Allah's need for writing—exalted be Allah far above that—rather, the text arrived in a manner fitting the realm of the Unseen. Thus, we believe in what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ informed us, without asking 'how', without anthropomorphism, resemblance, or specification.

Numerous authentic narrations have affirmed the inscription of this decree and its presence with Allah above the Throne.

Therefore, the expression is not an invitation to imagine the nature of the writing, the Book, or the Throne; rather, the lesson lies in the profound meaning carried by the news:

• The mercy of Allah is not an accidental matter.

• Nor is it a temporary impulse of pardon.

• Nor a passing state.

• It is among the greatest principles of His dealing with His creation.

Why was the Throne mentioned?

Then comes the phrase that shakes the soul:

"Which is with Him above the Throne."

The Throne is the greatest created entity Allah informed us about, and mentioning it in this context carries immense majesty.

However, one must not burden the text with philosophical symbols from oneself, nor assert a meaning not established by the text.

We believe in the Throne as revealed in the Book and Sunnah, believe in the divine news as it came, and entrust its true reality to Allah Almighty.

Nevertheless, the contemplating heart may stand at the majesty of the scene:

• The Throne in human consciousness is the symbol of sovereignty, greatness, and authority.

• The Book is with Him.

• In the Book is the word of Mercy.

• And Mercy is victorious over Anger.

It is as if the believer's heart, whenever the earth narrows around him, raises its gaze from earth to heaven, from the weakness of creation to the majesty of the Creator, and from the constriction of the moment to the vastness of Mercy.

The intent is not to reduce the Throne to a mere metaphorical symbol, for the text informs us of a real Throne; rather, we evoke the majesty of the scene and its spiritual impact without inquiring 'how' or making comparisons. Commentators of Hadith have cited various statements regarding the meaning of the Book and its location, while constantly emphasizing the affirmation of what came in the text without anthropomorphism.

O afflicted one... You are in Allah's mercy

Here the Hadith reaches the very heart of life.

To the man who sat alone after all doors were closed in his face.

To the woman who wept at night with no one listening to her cries.

To the sick person whose illness has lingered long.

To the poor person pressed hard by the world.

To the one who lost a beloved.

To the one waiting anxiously for news.

To the one carrying a grief he cannot explain to anyone.

To the one saying in the late hours of the night:

O Lord... until when?

To all of these, this Hadith comes like a gentle breeze from another realm:

My mercy overcame My anger.

But take heed...

This does not mean that every trial is a sign of divine pleasure, nor that every calamity is an inevitable expiation, nor that man understands the detailed wisdom behind everything that befalls him.

Yet the believer knows a grander truth:

That Allah does not exclude anything in His decree from His mercy and wisdom.

The trial may be painful...

Yet it is not proof that Allah has abandoned you.

The world may narrow around you...

Yet that does not mean Allah has forgotten you.

All doors may shut...

Yet Allah's door is never closed.

Trial can be a path, not a punishment

How many a person thought a calamity was his end, only to find it was the true beginning of his real life!

How many a person wept at a closed door, only to discover years later that Allah closed it out of mercy for him!

And how many a person lost something he loved, only to learn later that Allah was saving him from something he could not see!

We see the single moment...

While Allah knows the entire road.

We see the wound...

While Allah knows what lies beyond the wound.

We see the tear...

While Allah knows what that tear will build inside the heart.

We measure mercy by what we desire.

Whereas Allah Almighty shows us mercy through what He knows.

And here lies a grand secret:

Mercy may be in giving.

And mercy may be in withholding.

Mercy may be in delay.

Mercy may be in illness.

Mercy may be in loss.

And mercy may be in breaking something of your worldly life so that your bond with Allah remains unbroken.

So do not take pain as evidence of absence.

Destiny may cause you pain while Allah is with you.

You may weep while Allah knows.

Things may narrow while Allah opens.

And you may not understand while Allah fully knows.

The mercy you witness in your daily details

Just look around you...

How many times were you saved from something without realizing how close you were to it?

How many times was a matter delayed, only for you to discover that its delay was pure good?

How many times did you leave your home and return safely, without giving a moment's thought to thousands of dangers Allah turned away from you?

How many times did you fall ill and then recover?

How many times did you err and Allah covered your fault?

How many times were you about to make a terrible decision, then your heart was turned away from it?

How many times did you think your life was over, then relief arrived from a place you never anticipated?

These are not marginal details.

These are pages from a long book of mercy.

Page after page...

• Protection...

• Gentleness...

• Respite...

• Pardon...

• Sustenance...

• Salvation...

• Guidance...

• Repentance...

Until man may live long years immersed in Allah's blessings, yet fails to realize their worth until he loses some of them.

«﴿Do not despair of the mercy of Allah﴾»

From the greatest of mercies: Allah does not treat us according to all we deserve

If Allah treated us solely according to our deeds, who amongst us could stand?

Who among us has not erred?

Who among us has not fallen short?

Who among us has not slipped?

Who among us has not uttered a word he regretted?

Who among us has not delayed repentance?

Who among us has not felt his heart harden at times?

Yet Allah opens the door of seeking forgiveness.

He accepts repentance.

He replaces bad deeds with good deeds for whoever repents, believes, and performs righteous work.

And He proclaims:

«﴿Do not despair of the mercy of Allah﴾»

So do not make your sin greater than Allah's mercy.

Do not let your past be stronger than your repentance.

And do not allow Satan to convince you that you are unfit to return.

Mercy does not negate Justice

Here the believer must maintain a delicate balance.

If you say:

"Allah is Most Merciful, so I shall do as I please,"

You have misunderstood mercy.

And if you say:

"I am a sinner, so there is no hope for me,"

You have also misunderstood mercy.

True faith flies with two wings:

• Fear of Allah.

• Hope in Allah.

He fears so as not to become deluded.

And he hopes so as not to fall into despair.

He sins, then repents.

He stumbles, then rises.

He weeps, then marches forward.

And he never permits despair to dictate his future.

"Overcame"... A word for the future

Contemplate it once more:

OVERCAME.

The phrasing does not merely mean: Mercy exists.

Rather, the wording indicates precedence and victory.

This plants a magnificent truth in the heart:

Do not look at your life solely through the window of sin.

Nor solely through the window of affliction.

Nor solely through the window of failure.

For behind all of that is a Most Merciful Lord.

Behind every night is a dawn.

Behind every repentance is forgiveness.

Behind every return is closeness.

And behind every tear known to Allah is a restoration whose form we cannot fathom.

Even in the most grueling moments...

A person may sit in a small room while the whole world clamors around him.

He may possess no wealth,

No status,

No physical strength,

No human mediator,

And no one who understands what resides in his heart.

Then he raises his hands:

O Lord!

And this single word realigns the entire universe within him.

Because he knows there is a door that requires no appointment,

Needs no money,

Requires no human intercessor,

And is never shut in his face because of his poverty,

Nor because of his weakness,

Nor because of his brokenness.

It is the door of Allah.

Why was Mercy written down?

Because man forgets.

He forgets when worldly pressures intensify.

He forgets when relief is delayed.

He forgets when pain accumulates.

He forgets when he sees only the apparent and loses sight of the Unseen.

So the Hadith arrived to restore the truth to his heart:

Allah's mercy is not an invented concept man creates when seeking comfort; rather, it is divine news delivered by the Truthful Prophet ﷺ.

It was written.

It was established.

And it was proclaimed.

So do not let a moment of pain erase this supreme truth from your heart.

O you whose soul feels constrained...

If you are a sinner...

Repent.

If you are broken...

Turn to Him.

If you are tested...

Be patient and seek His help.

If you are poor...

Ask Him.

If you are confused...

Seek His guidance through Istikharah.

If you are distant...

Return.

And if you have fallen a hundred times...

Do not let the hundredth fall be the reason for abandoning standing up on the hundred and first time.

For your Lord is more merciful to you than you are to yourself.

And the door He opened for you when you were a sinner, do not close it yourself when you repent.

The Final Truth that should remain in the Heart

When the world narrows around you, do not look only at what you have lost.

Look at Him whom you have not lost.

ALLAH.

When people let you down, remember that the Lord of mankind never lets you down.

When doors are shut, remember that heaven's door needs no key from human hands.

When you see yourself weak, remember that your true strength is not within yourself.

When trial lingers, do not say:

"Why did Allah abandon me?"

Rather say:

"How does Allah wish to nurture me through this trial? And how can I draw closer to Him while enduring it?"

Then elevate your heart to the ultimate truth inscribed in the grand prophetic report:

«Verily, My mercy overcame My anger»

It is not a license for sin...

But a call to repentance.

It is not a promise of immunity from reckoning...

But the opening of the door of hope.

It is not an annulment of justice...

But a manifestation of the vastness of mercy.

It is not an invitation to abandon effort...

But a call to strive with a heart that knows no despair.

O human being...

If life hurts you, do not think mercy has vanished.

Mercy may be with you while you do not perceive it.

It may lie in the path you did not choose.

It may be in a door that was closed.

It may be in a person who moved away.

It may be in an illness that slowed your pace.

It may be in provision that was delayed.

It may be in a tear that cleansed your heart.

And it may be in a prostration you would not have lengthened were it not for the pain.

How many an ordeal sprouted faith...

How many a tear engendered certainty...

How many a brokenness taught the heart the essence of humbleness before Allah...

And how many a door closed on earth became the reason for opening a door in heaven!

Thus the believer marches on...

He does not deny pain, yet he does not worship pain.

He does not deny fear, yet he does not surrender to it.

He does not deny sin, yet he never despairs.

He does not deny trial, yet he sees behind it a Wise, Merciful Lord.

And deep within his heart remains a light that storms cannot extinguish:

MY MERCY OVERCAME MY ANGER

So if you know your Lord through this mercy, do not flee from Him when you sin...

Rather flee unto Him.

If He tests you...

Remain steadfast with Him.

If He breaks you...

Prostrate before Him.

And if the world grows dark...

Do not extinguish the light of hope in your heart.

For He who wrote mercy...

Is He who fully knows your weakness.

And He who knows your weakness...

Is He who opened for you the door of repentance.

And He who opened for you the door of repentance...

Did not intend for you to despair.

Rather, He intended for you to return.
