When the Embroidery Fades… and the Garment Remains: The Estrangement of Monotheism in the End of Time

✍ Ali Khalil Abbas Al-Seljuki Al-Turkmani Al-Lailani Al-Seljuki Meditative Encyclopedia
"Islam will fade away as the embroidery of a garment fades away, until it will not be known what fasting, prayer, rituals, or charity are. The Book of Allah, the Mighty and Sublime, will be taken away in a single night, and not one verse of it will remain on earth. Groups of people will remain—the old man and the old woman—saying: 'We found our fathers upon this word: La ilaha illallah (There is no god but Allah), so we say it.'"

When the Light Begins to Fade

There are Hadiths that do not merely pass through the heart like ordinary words, but rather descend upon it like thunderbolts; awakening it from its heedlessness, revealing what lies behind the external appearances of life regarding divine laws, and what lies behind the noise of days regarding destinies.

Among these Hadiths is that majestic prophetic scene:

"Islam will fade away as the embroidery of a garment fades away."

It is an imagery so profound that you can hardly find anything more eloquent in portraying the fading of religion within souls and societies.

The embroidery (Washi) represents the beauty of the garment, its decoration, and its patterns that grant it its luster, prestige, and uniqueness. The garment itself may remain a garment, but the embroidery fades; the piece of cloth may remain standing, but its beauty departs, its details disappear, and its colors are extinguished, until the onlooker can no longer see what he used to see in it yesterday.

Herein lies the secret of the deep prophetic imagery:

A thing may not fall all at once, but rather its structure may remain while its spirit departs, its name may remain while its reality is lost, and its shape may remain while its embroidery wears away.

And thus begins the fading of Islam in the lives of people.

The story does not usually begin with the destruction of mosques.

Nor with the burning of copies of the Quran.

Nor with declaring war on religion.

Rather, it begins with something quieter and far more dangerous:

With the changing of the observing eye.

The standards of beauty change.

The measures of ugliness shift.

And what the heart used to see as light becomes… old-fashioned.

And what it used to see as modesty becomes… backwardness.

And what it used to see as chastity becomes… a complex.

And what it used to see as obedience becomes… weakness.

And what it used to see as Monotheism (Tawheed) becomes… narrow-mindedness.

And what it used to see as commitment becomes… extremism.

Then a generation grows up that does not necessarily hate religion, but no longer sees its beauty.

And this is the most dangerous stage of fading.

The Embroidery First… Then the Garment

Contemplate the garment.

The garment is one thing, and the embroidery is another.

The garment may be present, but its decoration departs.

And thus the name of Islam can remain, some of its rituals can remain, some of its terms can remain, and some of its manifestations can remain, but the embroidery of religion wears away little by little.

Voluntary traditions (Sunan) recede.

Then obligatory duties weaken within souls.

Then worship turns into habit.

Then the permissible (Halal) and impermissible (Haram) become mere inherited terms.

Then the meaning of manhood changes.

And the meaning of womanhood changes.

And the meaning of family changes.

And the meaning of modesty changes.

And the meaning of trustworthiness changes.

And the meaning of honor changes.

And the meaning of success changes.

And the meaning of happiness changes.

Then man becomes a Muslim by name, but he derives his life standards from outside of Islam.

Here the embroidery begins to disappear.

For the most dangerous thing to afflict nations is not to lose their beliefs all at once, but to lose their sensitivity toward losing them.

A stitch falls from the garment and no one notices.

Then another stitch.

Then a color.

Then a pattern.

Then comes a day when people look at the garment and do not remember that it was once decorated.

The Tragedy of Man Is Not in the Garment Alone… But in the Eye

Beauty does not always disappear from the object; sometimes the eye of the observer changes.

And this is one of the most critical issues of our era.

Modesty may be beautiful in itself, but if the eye is corrupted, modesty becomes weakness.

Dignity may be beautiful, but if the eye is corrupted, dignity becomes backwardness.

Simplicity may be beautiful, but if the eye is corrupted, its possessor becomes poor in meaning.

Chastity may be a crown, but if the eye is corrupted, the chaste becomes an object of mockery.

Night prayer (Qiyam al-Layl) may be life for the heart, but if the eye is corrupted, its performer becomes a stranger.

Trustworthiness may be an honor, but if the eye is corrupted, the trustworthy becomes unable to keep up with the times.

Silence may be wisdom, but if the eye is corrupted, the wise falls behind the noise of the market.

Thus falsehood does not always need to kill the truth.

It is sometimes enough for it to redefine things.

So the ugly becomes beautiful.

And the beautiful becomes ugly.

And truthfulness becomes naivety.

And lying becomes a skill.

And modesty becomes a complex.

And insolence becomes boldness.

And humility becomes weakness.

And arrogance becomes personality.

And asceticism becomes failure.

And extravagance becomes success.

And chastity becomes deprivation.

And betrayal becomes intelligence.

And trustworthiness becomes stupidity.

Here the danger is not merely that people left the embroidery; but that they no longer see the embroidery as beautiful.

And Thus Meanings Fade Before Shapes Fade

Religion does not live in copies of the Quran alone, nor in mosques alone, nor in the words moved by tongues.

Religion is life.

Religion is a scale.

Religion is a conscience.

Religion is a stance.

Religion is to see Allah before you see people.

Religion is for the truth to be heavier in your heart than self-interest.

Religion is to say "No" when the whole world says "Yes" to falsehood.

Religion is for the forbidden to have ugliness inside you, even if the forbidden becomes familiar.

And for the permissible to have beauty in your heart, even if people mock it.

"For indeed, it is not eyes that are blinded, but blinded are the hearts which are within the breasts."

So if hearts are blinded, they no longer see the embroidery.

And if insight is lost, the decorated garment becomes in its eye less valuable than a torn rag, because the scales themselves have collapsed.

History Tells Us About the Fall of Nations

When we read history, we find that civilizations do not always collapse when their walls are destroyed, but rather they may collapse while they are still standing.

Values fall before fortresses fall.

Will falls before armies fall.

And meaning dies before the body dies.

How many a nation possessed palaces, markets, and armies, yet lost the very thing that makes power power, civilization civilization, and man man.

And how many a nation small in number and poor in means possessed within itself a meaning greater than everything its opponents possessed.

This is the difference between material life and spiritual faith life.

Man may own the entire world, yet not own himself.

And man may lose much of the world, yet own his heart.

And the Quran places the scale in a single word:

"He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]."

The Embroidery Is the Beauty of Monotheism

And there is no greater embroidery upon the garment of Islam than Monotheism (Tawheed).

La ilaha illallah (There is no god but Allah).

Two words upon the tongue, yet they are greater than the world and everything in it.

They are a declaration of liberating man from the servitude of everything other than Allah.

Not a slave to money.

Nor a slave to desire.

Nor a slave to people.

Nor a slave to power.

Nor a slave to tribe.

Nor a slave to the audience.

Nor a slave to an image he created for himself before others.

It is a word that shifts the center of life from earth to heaven.

And from the creation to the Creator.

And from desire to divine revelation.

And from fear of people to fear of Allah.

And from attachment to secondary causes to attachment to the Causer of causes.

That is why Monotheism was the foundation of Islam, its spirit, and the key to its message.

"So know that there is no deity except Allah."

"The Book of Allah, the Mighty and Sublime, will be taken away in a single night, and not one verse of it will remain on earth."

But Even the Embroidery Fades…

The Hadith takes the soul toward a scene of even greater awe.

The embroidery fades.

Then the landmarks depart.

Then knowledge becomes rare.

Then people forget prayer.

And forget fasting.

And forget obligatory charity (Zakat).

And forget rituals.

Until the matter reaches a stage where nothing is known of these rituals except the name.

Here a terrifying truth appears:

Knowledge is not guaranteed forever.

A generation knows.

And a generation learns from it.

And a generation imitates.

Then comes a generation that does not ask.

Then comes a generation that does not know why it does what it does.

Then comes a generation that does not know at all what its fathers used to do.

Therefore, the greatest responsibility in every home is not merely for a father to leave money for his children.

Rather, to leave them the meaning.

To teach his son why he prays.

And why he fasts.

And why he lowers his gaze.

And why he guards his tongue.

And why he speaks the truth.

And why he does not oppress.

And why he does not sell his religion for the worldly gain of others.

For if you preserve the name for your son and lose the meaning, you have left him a shell without a kernel.

And if you teach him a profession and lose him from his Lord, you have given him a means to earn a living without giving him a reason to live.

Then Comes the Greatest Scene: The Lifting of the Quran

"The Book of Allah, the Mighty and Sublime, will be taken away in a single night, and not one verse of it will remain on earth."

What a scene!

A Book that was in millions of breasts.

And its verses were recited in mosques.

And homes opened their days with it.

And hearts trembled upon hearing it.

Then comes a day when not a single verse remains of it.

Not in the breast of a memorizer.

Nor in a copy of the Quran.

Nor on the tongue of a reciter.

As if heaven had closed the last window of light.

Here man feels the value of the blessing in his hands right now.

The copy of the Quran on the shelf is not a piece of paper.

And the verse you hear from your phone is not just a sound.

And the ability to read Al-Fatiha is not an ordinary matter.

It is a blessing.

And that you can say: "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds" and know its meaning… is a blessing.

And that you can prostrate and say: "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High" while knowing to Whom you prostrate… is a blessing.

And that you know the permissible from the forbidden… is a blessing.

And that something remains in your heart telling you: "This pleases Allah, and this angers Allah"… is among Allah's greatest blessings upon you.

Then Remains the Last Thread

Yet after all this darkness, something remains.

A word remains.

La ilaha illallah (There is no god but Allah).

What a word!

When the whole world weakens, it remains.

When decorations fall, it remains.

When standards shift, it remains.

When people forget details, it remains.

And when the embroidery departs, the origin of the garment remains.

Yet the Hadith does not call for relying upon a mere phrase without reality; for Monotheism in its origin is faith in Allah and sincerity to Him, not merely sounds uttered by lips.

Rather, the majesty of the scene lies in that the last thing remaining in some people is the core knowledge of Allah, that natural disposition (Fitrah) they received from their fathers, when ignorance prevails and knowledge is cut off.

Here Allah's mercy manifests.

A mercy that does not waste from His servants whatever core faith remains in their hearts.

Do Not Be Deceived by the Abundance of Colors

Man may look at his era and say: The world is full of religion!

Many mosques.

Many copies of the Quran.

Many sermons.

Many posts.

Many channels.

Many words.

Yet the deeper question is:

How much of the embroidery remains?

How much of the effect of Monotheism remains in manners?

How much of it remains in markets?

How much of it remains in politics?

How much of it remains in the family?

How much of it remains in raising children?

How much of it remains when human oversight is absent?

How much of it remains when self-interest is on one side and truth is on the other?

Here true religion appears.

Not when people applaud you.

But when you can oppose your desire for Allah.

Not when you are in the mosque only.

But when you enter the market.

Not when you talk about piety.

But when an opportunity for the forbidden is placed before you and no one sees you.

There the embroidery appears.

Modern Man… and the New Vision

Contemporary man has created for himself a world of images.

A screen… a screen… money… position… appearance… luxury… fame… admiration… image.

Then man began measuring his value by what people see of him, not by what Allah sees of him.

Here the great shift occurred.

The question became: How do I look? Instead of: Who am I with Allah?

The question became: What do people say about me? Instead of: What does Allah say about me?

The criterion of success became: How much do I own? How much do I show? How many people follow me? How many applaud me?

While the spiritual scale used to ask: How much do you fear Allah? How truthful are you? How much do you forgive? How much do you preserve trust? How much do you resist your desire? How much do you remember Allah? How much good do you carry for people?

Here the eye changes. And when the eye changes… the embroidery disappears.

Do Not Be Among Those Who Cannot See Beauty

Man may pass by the Dawn Prayer (Fajr) and see nothing in it but the fatigue of waking up.

And pass by the Quran and see nothing in it but pages.

And pass by charity and see nothing in it but a decrease in wealth.

And pass by chastity and see nothing in it but deprivation.

And pass by modesty and see nothing in it but restrictions.

And pass by dutifulness to parents and see nothing in it but a heavy duty.

Yet the believing heart sees something else.

It sees in Fajr an appointment with Allah.

And sees in the Quran a message from heaven.

And sees in charity a purification of soul and wealth.

And sees in chastity dignity.

And sees in modesty beauty.

And sees in dutifulness to parents a door to Paradise.

That is the embroidery. The embroidery is not always in the things themselves. It is in the eye of the heart that beholds them.

The Most Dangerous Moment: When Falsehood Becomes Familiar

The most dangerous thing about trial is not that it comes to you shouting: I am a trial!

Rather, that it comes to you in an ordinary form.

Then repeats itself. Then you become accustomed to it. Then you fall silent about it. Then you justify it. Then you defend it. Then you wonder at the one who denounces it.

And at that point, trial has succeeded.

For when you see an evil for the first time, it shakes you. Then if it repeats before you, it becomes light. Then if it becomes light, it becomes familiar. Then if it becomes familiar, it becomes acceptable. Then if it becomes acceptable, it becomes a standard. Then the one who denounces it becomes the stranger!

Here we understand the meaning of estrangement. The stranger is not necessarily the one whom people do not know. Rather, the stranger may be the one who remained seeing the beauty that people lost the ability to see.

O Stranger in a Time of Estrangement

Blessed are those in whose hearts something of the embroidery remains.

Blessed are those whose hearts still tremble upon hearing the Quran.

Blessed are those who, when they see the forbidden, say: No.

Blessed are those who, when people oppose them, say: But Allah sees.

Blessed are those who did not make the multitude of travelers proof of the truth.

And blessed are those who did not make the spread of falsehood a reason to deem it good.

And blessed are those whose hearts remained knowing their Lord.

For truth does not become falsehood because people abandoned it. And falsehood does not become truth because people followed it.

And beauty does not lose its beauty because eyes are blinded to it. And the embroidery does not lose its value because people preferred the plain garment.

Preserve the Stitch in Your Hand

The Hadith "Islam will fade away as the embroidery of a garment fades away" is not a call to despair. Nor is it a call for the believer to sit waiting for the ruin of the world.

It is a call: Preserve what you can.

Preserve your prayer. Preserve your heart. Preserve your tongue. Preserve your home. Preserve your children. Preserve the Quran. Preserve Monotheism. Preserve manners. Preserve modesty. Preserve trustworthiness. Preserve the meaning of being a servant to Allah.

Every prostration is a stitch. Every verse is a stitch. Every charity is a stitch. Every repentance is a stitch. Every stance of truth is a stitch. Every resistance to desire is a stitch. Every word of truth is a stitch. Every pardon with ability is a stitch. Every tear of fear of Allah is a stitch.

And every hand extending toward good is sewing upon this garment whose embroidery has begun to fade.

As for the Garment… Do Not Despise It

The embroidery may depart. Colors may weaken. Decorations may fall. But do not despise the origin of the garment.

Monotheism (Tawheed). La ilaha illallah.

It is the word brought by Adam. It is the word of Noah. It is the call of Abraham. It is the message of the prophets. And it is the core of the call of Muhammad ﷺ.

"And We sent not before you any messenger except that We revealed to him that, 'There is no deity except Me, so worship Me.'"

It is not a historical slogan. It is the grand truth that, when it settles in the heart, rearranges the entire universe.

Allah first. Allah last. Allah above everything. Allah is greater than everything.

And If the Embroidery Departs… Then the Garment Departs

Then comes the end of the scene.

After the embroidery wears away, the garment itself wears away.

And here the prophetic depiction reaches its ultimate end.

When there remains no Quran on earth, no prayer, no fasting, no Zakat, no rituals, and no real knowledge of religion… then the era of message on earth has ended, and there remains no meaning for the continuation of the world upon which it was established.

This places us before a grand truth: The existence of faith among us is not guaranteed forever.

It is a blessing that can be lost. Knowledge can be lifted. Hearts can change. And generations can forget.

So do not say: Tomorrow I will learn. Tomorrow I will repent. Tomorrow I will memorize the Quran. Tomorrow I will raise my children. Tomorrow I will return to Allah.

For you do not own tomorrow. You own this moment. This prostration. This verse. This repentance. This heart. This opportunity.

Conclusion: Do Not Let the Embroidery Fall from Your Hands

O Son of Adam…

Look at the garment in your hands. Look at your religion. Look at your heart. What remains in it?

Does prayer still have its beauty? Does the Quran still have its awe? Does modesty still have its light? Does truthfulness still have its taste? Does charity still have its joy? Does Monotheism still have its authority?

Does "La ilaha illallah" still stir your depths, or has it become a word passing over the tongue without descending into the heart?

For our greatest fear is not that religion departs from the world suddenly. But that it departs from our hearts little by little while we do not realize.

That a stitch falls today and we do not care. Tomorrow another. And the day after another. Until we look at the garment and do not recognize that it was once beautiful.

O Lord… Do not make us among those who lost the ability to see the beauty of the embroidery. Do not make us among those who became accustomed to falsehood until they saw it as truth. Nor among those who saw modesty as weakness, chastity as backwardness, obedience as a restriction, and sin as freedom.

O Allah, keep in our hearts the light of Monotheism. O Allah, keep in our homes the Quran. O Allah, keep in our manners the trace of prophethood. O Allah, make us among those who, when the embroidery faded, preserved the garment. And when estrangement intensified, stood firm. And when misguidance increased, did not go astray. And when standards shifted, the scale of Allah remained in their hearts. And if people say: "This is beauty," they say: "Rather, beauty is what Allah beautified." And if people say: "This is freedom," they say: "Rather, freedom is to be a servant to Allah, so no desire, money, authority, or human enslaves you." And if people say: "This is life," they say: "Rather, life is the life of the heart."

"Is he who was dead and We gave him life and made for him light by which to walk among the people like one who is in darknesses, never to come out thereof?"

O you in whose hand something of the embroidery remains… do not leave it. And O you in whose heart something of the light remains… do not extinguish it. And O you who knew Allah… do not replace Him with anyone.

And O you who said: "La ilaha illallah", let it be a life not a word, a methodology not a slogan, and a scale not a memory.

For perhaps the greatest thing you possess in a time when faces, scales, and tastes shift… is to remain able to see the beauty of the garment whose beauty people almost forgot.
